01Data & insight

Business Intelligence & Data Visualisation

University Assessment | Power BI · Excel · DAX

Project Overview

The project addressed the need to make a large emissions dataset easier to explore and compare. Developed an interactive Power BI report examining UK CO₂ emissions and population trends between 1990 and 2023. Worked with two Excel datasets containing 7,585 emissions records and 232 country records, organising data and developing analytical visualisations to communicate key trends.

My Role

Prepared and structured datasets, developed data relationships and DAX measures, and created report visuals covering UK annual emissions, emissions by fuel source, annual reductions, emissions per person, population changes and comparisons with Germany and France.

Emissions dataset records
7,585
Country dataset records
232

Key Findings

UK CO₂ emissions fell substantially between 1990 and 2023, reaching approximately 307.83 million tonnes in 2023, or 4.48 tonnes per person. Emissions declined despite UK population growth, while coal-related emissions fell markedly. The country comparison showed that the UK's absolute 2023 emissions were lower than Germany's but higher than France's. These national trends provide context rather than measurements of the NHS Trust's own emissions.

Outcome: An interactive report brings trends, country comparisons and emissions-per-person measures into one place. It supports exploration of the supplied data; no real-world NHS emissions reduction or operational impact is claimed.

02Business & Market Research

UK Charity Sector Research & Outreach Strategy

PurposeTech.ai | Analytics & Research Internship | Microsoft Excel

During my Analytics & Research Internship at PurposeTech.ai, conducted research into the UK charity grant-funding market to understand fundraising challenges, digital adoption and opportunities for more targeted stakeholder engagement. Structured the findings across four Excel worksheets, connecting secondary research to practical charity segmentation and outreach planning.

My Work / Four Worksheets

  1. 01

    Collected evidence from sector reports, charity research websites, grant discovery platforms and webinars. Recorded themes, source links, insights, emerging patterns and potential outreach applications.

  2. 02

    Questions Summary

    Synthesised answers to seven questions covering grant application challenges, time-consuming tasks, funding discovery, tools, common frustrations, AI adoption and fundraising trends.

  3. 03

    Developed six targeted variants with qualification criteria, audience descriptions, messaging angles, personalised email subjects, initial email drafts and follow-up variations.

  4. 04

    Verification Criteria

    Established checks for charity registration, organisation size, cause area, relevant job titles, website and LinkedIn evidence, grant activity and exclusion criteria to distinguish suitable prospects from weak matches.

Key Findings / Secondary Research

Findings drawn from the consulted sources and recorded in the workbook; these are not results from an independently conducted charity survey.

  • Fundraising capacity

    Sector research highlighted limited staff and resources at smaller charities, making funding research and repeated applications particularly demanding (Amar & Ramsay, 2026).

  • Application friction

    Grant discovery, eligibility checks and differing funder requirements contribute to repetitive administrative work.

  • Funding pressure

    Charities Aid Foundation reported estimated public donations of £15.4 billion in 2024 (2025) and £14 billion in 2025 (2026). This provides context for the project's focus on fundraising pressures.

  • AI adoption

    The workbook used interim 2026 research reporting 88% AI adoption (Amar & Ramsay, 2026). The final Charity Digital Skills Report (Amar & Ramsay, 2026) revised the figure to 79%, compared with 76% in 2025, covering exploring, active and strategic adoption. The interim figure should not be treated as the final sector estimate or a measure of everyday use.

  • Existing market tools

    GrantFinder (n.d.) helps users find funding opportunities, while 360Giving (n.d.) provides searchable data about grants already awarded. Their different purposes matter when considering which tools could support prospect research.

03Restaurant digital transformation

Restaurant Digital Transformation

Ember & Grain — A Digital Dining Experience

University Assessment | HTML · CSS · JavaScript

The project

Designed and developed a responsive restaurant website prototype as part of a digital transformation scenario. Acting as the restaurant's CTO, the objective was to improve customer communication, simplify booking enquiries and bring essential information together in one accessible digital experience.

Fictional university assessment scenario.

  • 01

    Responsive Website

    A layout designed for desktop, tablet and mobile visitors.

  • 02

    Customer Enquiry Form

    A structured form collecting customer details, enquiry type and optional booking preferences.

  • 03

    Email Draft Generation

    JavaScript generates a formatted email preview from the user's input.

  • 04

    Accessible Contact Information

    Restaurant contact details and clearly labelled demonstration social media integration.

My role / Business problem

Restaurant information and customer communication need to be more accessible, booking enquiries easier to prepare, and contact details and social media clearly presented.

I planned and developed the prototype using HTML5 and CSS3, with JavaScript generating formatted email drafts. Usability, responsive design and clear navigation informed the interface.

Design rationale

Dark premium colours and elegant typography establish a distinctive restaurant identity. Clear visual hierarchy guides visitors towards the enquiry form, while responsive layouts support phones and smaller devices.

Clearly labelled fields and validation reduce confusion. Contact information and demonstration social media details are easy to locate.

Outcome & limitations

A responsive, client-side restaurant website prototype demonstrating how web development can support customer communication. The form generates a copyable email draft and offers an optional email-app link; it does not automatically send email or confirm reservations.

No database, payment system or backend is implemented. Restaurant contact information and social profiles are demonstration details; the fictional recipient is not a working inbox.
